# Amendment — CLM-013-25 claim text (2026-10-20)

**Decision:** the spec owner (Tristen Pierson), 2026-10-20 — amend the
claim text to match the shipped two-class rule. The alternative,
extending the implementation so organisation references are also
resolution-checked, was considered and not chosen.

**Where this leaves the record:** claims bundle v0.2.1 (was v0.2.0).
No code and no test changed. The after-implement assessment anchored
externally on 2026-10-20 (`aee-assessment-after-implement.json`,
Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23489995) stands unchanged as the record of
the pre-amendment bundle; the amended bundle is re-assessed in
`aee-assessment-after-implement-2026-10-20-clm-013-25-amendment.json`
and anchored as a new version under the same Zenodo concept.

## The old text (pre-amendment, verbatim)

> SPRS self-assessment input validation requires each evidence link to
> resolve: the referenced target must exist and identify as claimed
> (hash or identity match). A well-formed but non-resolving link fails
> validation, so the computed self-score cannot be moved by operator
> assertion alone.

## The new text (verbatim)

> SPRS self-assessment input validation treats evidence links by
> class. A link that names a platform record — a POA&M item, an audit
> export, or a legal hold recorded in the same scope, or an anchor
> into a document the platform maintains — must resolve: the named
> target must exist, and a document anchor must name the very
> requirement the link is offered as evidence for. A link that does
> not resolve is refused when the state is written, and one whose
> target later stops resolving is a named failure in the computed
> report. Any other single-token reference is the organisation's own
> evidence: it is recorded and reported as operator-attested, labelled
> as such wherever it is reported, and never represented as resolved.
> A value that is prose rather than a reference is refused, so the
> computed self-score cannot be moved by operator assertion alone.

## Why

The old text promised resolution for *every* evidence link. What
shipped — Spec 005's designed rule, recorded in that package's
qualification note of 2026-10-13 and confirmed by the acceptance
battery (probe p12 and supplements p12c/p12d) — is a two-class rule:
links that claim platform records are resolution-checked and refused
at write time when they do not resolve; references to an
organisation's own records cannot be checked by the platform, so they
are recorded and reported as operator-attested, never as resolved.
The battery measured the gap and the T40 assessment scored the claim
0.250 (band low, below the 0.70 threshold) with a `contradicts`
evidence entry describing it; Spec 013's after-implement outcome was
CLARIFY for that reason alone. The owner's ruling settles the wording
in favour of the shipped, designed behaviour.

The new text states exactly what the implementation does — verified
against `src/tekmerion/platform/compliance.py` and
`tests/test_e7_redteam_platform.py::test_p12_sprs_evidence_links_must_resolve`
— no more and no less.

## What changed in the bundle

- **Text** replaced as above.
- **The former `contradicts` evidence entry is kept, annotated, and
  reclassified to `supports`.** It was accurate against the old text;
  its ref now carries a dated annotation saying so, and the original
  wording is preserved inside it. Under the amended text the same
  battery measurement is confirming evidence, which is why the
  direction changes; the entry is not deleted, and this note is the
  public record of the episode.
- **One evidence entry added**, recording this decision and the
  amendment.
- **Falsifier F-013-25 updated** to test the amended text: it now
  also fails if assertion prose is accepted, if a non-resolving
  platform-form link is accepted at write time or goes unnamed when
  its target stops resolving, or if an organisation reference is ever
  reported as resolved.
- **Status** moved to supported / checked after the re-assessment
  below (it was draft).
